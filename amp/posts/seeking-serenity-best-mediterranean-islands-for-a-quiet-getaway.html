For pure tranquility, I'd highly recommend Folegandros in Greece. It's a Cycladic island but much quieter than Santorini or Mykonos. The Chora is stunning, perched on a cliff, and the beaches are ruggedly beautiful and often empty. The food is simple, fresh, and delicious. It's perfect for unwinding.

If you're open to something a bit different, consider the Aeolian Islands off the coast of Sicily. Salina, in particular, is known for its capers and wine, and it's wonderfully peaceful. Stromboli has its active volcano, which is an experience, but the other islands offer serious serenity. You can find some amazing local trattorias there.

You absolutely must look into Paxos and Antipaxos in the Ionian Sea, Greece. Paxos is small and charming with olive groves and hidden coves, while Antipaxos boasts some of the most incredible turquoise waters I've ever seen. They are relatively undeveloped and perfect for a quiet escape. The seafood is out of this world!

For a taste of authentic Croatia away from the crowds, consider the island of Vis. It was a military base for years, so it's remained less developed. The main town is lovely, and there are secluded beaches and crystal-clear water. The local wine is also a big draw. It feels like stepping back in time.

Sardinia has many less-visited corners. Instead of the popular Costa Smeralda, explore the southern coast or the smaller islands like San Pietro. It has a unique Pescatorian culture, stunning beaches, and delicious seafood. It's a great place to disconnect.

I recently discovered Alonissos in Greece, part of the Sporades. It's a marine park, so it's very protected and incredibly green. The old village (Chora) is beautiful, and the beaches are pristine. It's a haven for nature lovers and those seeking peace.

Consider Korčula in Croatia. While it has a beautiful walled old town, the rest of the island is dotted with vineyards, olive groves, and quiet bays. It's much more relaxed than Dubrovnik or Split. The local Grk wine is a must-try.

For a truly remote feel, look at the smaller Dodecanese islands like Symi or Halki in Greece. Symi is known for its stunning neoclassical architecture, but it's small and can be very peaceful, especially outside peak season. Halki is even smaller and has a very laid-back vibe. Navigating tipping in smaller European towns can be tricky, so it might be worth checking out Navigating Tipping Etiquette Across Europe: A Guide for Travelers before you go.
